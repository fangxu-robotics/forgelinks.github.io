Weather RNN Playground — 2 inputs · 3 hidden neurons · a real forecast

Each day the network reads two numbers — temperature and humidity — updates its 3-neuron memory h, and guesses tomorrow’s weather. Same idea as before, but x, h and y are now vectors, and the weights are matrices. Step through the week and check every guess against what actually happened.

The rolled view — one cell, now with vectors flowing through it

Two numbers enter, three memory numbers circulate, two numbers leave. The arrows carry whole vectors; the weights are matrices whose shapes make the sizes match.

ht = tanh( Wx·xt + Wh·ht−1 + b )
ŷt = Wy·ht + by  ← tomorrow’s (T, H), scaled
Exactly the scalar rules, upgraded to matrices: Wx is 3×2 (2 inputs feed 3 neurons), Wh is 3×3 (3 memories feed 3 neurons), Wy is 2×3 (3 memories are read out into 2 predictions). Temperatures are scaled as (T−15)/10 and humidity as (H−60)/25 so everything lives near ±1.

Three experiments to try

1The forecasting ladder. On Warming week, Play through with Always average, then Copy today, then Trained. Watch the avg-miss badge drop each time — that gap is exactly what learning bought.
2Break the memory. With Trained weights, set all 9 numbers of Wh to 0. The network still sees today, but forgets every yesterday — the rising trend gets missed and the miss grows. Wh is where “momentum” lives.
3Catch the turn. On Cold snap, compare Copy today vs Trained around Days 2–4 (hover the prediction cards). Copying yesterday is always one day late at a turning point.
